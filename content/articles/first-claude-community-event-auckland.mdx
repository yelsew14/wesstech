> **Overview**
>
> I recently attended my first Claude community event in Auckland, organised by **TheColab.ai**. As a Manager – Site Reliability Engineering (SRE), I wanted to hear how professionals are applying AI in real enterprise environments. The evening demonstrated that successful AI adoption depends as much on collaboration and community as it does on technology.

# What I Learned From My First Claude Community Event in Auckland

Artificial intelligence is evolving rapidly, and one of the best ways to keep pace is by learning directly from practitioners. That was my motivation for attending my first Claude community event in Auckland.

The event brought together people from different industries who shared practical experiences, lessons learned, and ideas for using AI responsibly.

## A Community Built Around Learning

Instead of a product presentation, the event encouraged discussion. Participants openly explored opportunities, challenges, governance, security, and real-world adoption of AI.

## Claude as an Engineering Assistant

Many conversations focused on using Claude to improve productivity through documentation, coding assistance, summarisation, brainstorming, and research.

As an engineering leader, I see these capabilities reducing repetitive work while allowing teams to spend more time solving meaningful business problems.

## My Perspective as an SRE Manager

Modern Site Reliability Engineering is about reducing operational toil through automation while improving reliability and resilience.

AI has the potential to support those goals by assisting with documentation, scripting, troubleshooting, and knowledge sharing. Used responsibly, it complements engineers rather than replacing them.

## Responsible AI Adoption

Enterprise organisations should balance innovation with governance. Human review, security, privacy, and organisational policies remain essential as AI becomes embedded in everyday workflows.

## Why Local AI Communities Matter

Communities accelerate learning. Hearing directly from practitioners provides perspectives that cannot always be gained through documentation alone.

As Auckland's AI ecosystem continues to grow, these events create valuable opportunities to connect, collaborate, and learn together.

## Final Thoughts

Attending my first Claude community event reinforced that AI is ultimately about people working together to solve problems. Technology will continue to evolve, but communities will remain one of the most valuable ways to learn and share practical experience.

## Key Takeaways

- AI communities encourage practical learning.
- Claude is becoming a valuable productivity assistant.
- Responsible AI requires governance and human oversight.
- Engineering leaders should focus on business value, not just technology.
